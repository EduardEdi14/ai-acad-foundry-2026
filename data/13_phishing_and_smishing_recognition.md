# How to Recognize a Phishing or Smishing Attempt

Phishing (fraudulent email) and smishing (fraudulent SMS) are the two most common ways criminals try to reach Libra Assist customers. Both rely on urgency and a fake sense of authority.

## Warning Signs
1. The message pressures you to act **within minutes or hours** ("your account will be closed today").
2. It asks you to click a link and log in on a page that looks like Libra Assist but has a different or misspelled web address.
3. It asks you to read out or type an OTP code, your PIN, or your Internet Banking password.
4. The sender address is a public email domain (e.g. gmail.com) or a number you do not recognize, not an official Libra Assist channel.

## What Libra Assist Will Never Do
* Ask for your PIN, CVV2/CVC2 code, or full Internet Banking password, by phone, SMS, or email.
* Ask you to install a "security" application from a link sent by SMS.
* Ask you to move money to a "safe account" to protect it.

## Step-by-Step: If You Suspect a Phishing or Smishing Message
1. Do not click any link and do not reply to the message.
2. Do not enter any credentials on the page the message links to, even if it looks identical to Libra Assist.
3. If you already entered data on the suspicious page, open the mobile app immediately and block the affected card yourself, or call the emergency line at **021.999.000**.
4. Forward the suspicious SMS or email to **phishing@libraassist.example** so the fraud team can block the sender.
5. Delete the message after reporting it.
